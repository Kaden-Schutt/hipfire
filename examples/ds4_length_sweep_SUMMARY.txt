ANSWER: The quants score ~9 at 128 (mq2r 9.30 / lloyd 8.73) while the parent scores 23.64.
The parent is ALREADY broken at 128 tokens. Every oracle/bisect run at 128 was correctly aimed
at a defective model — the defect is NOT something that only appears as the sequence grows past 128.

The gap is NOT flat: parent PPL rises 23.6 → 29.6 → 63.5 → 163.9 while quants only drift
9.3 → 11.1 → 11.7 → 14.7. Absolute parent−quant gap grows ~10× (14.3 → 149.2), and parent-vs-quant
KLD mean rises 5.30 → 7.68 with top-1 falling 0.44 → 0.30. So long-seq decay is real on top of
an already-present short-seq defect. Position buckets confirm parent trails quants from [32,64)
onward (0.54 vs 0.63–0.71) and keeps losing ground at longer buckets.

Quant-vs-quant control: KLD mean 0.160/0.149/0.130/0.106, top1 0.867/0.859/0.867/0.873 —
matches the known 0.102/0.873 at 1024. Quants agree with each other at every length.

Implication for the investigation: the clean per-layer bisect at 128 is the real puzzle.
The search must explain why layer/handoff oracles look clean while end-to-end PPL is already 2.5× worse.
Do NOT abandon 128-token oracles; do NOT treat the defect as length-onset-only.
