# Redline: taking MQ4R decode from 110 to 204 tok/s on gfx12

**Workload:** Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B MQ4R

**Hardware:** Radeon AI PRO R9700 (`gfx1201`)

**Runtime:** ROCm 7.2

**Mode:** ordinary autoregressive decode, Q8 KV, no MTP or speculative decoding

**Clock policy:** automatic

**Campaign:** approximately July 1–12, 2026

## Result

In roughly a week and a half, the gfx12 MQ4R autoregressive path improved from
approximately **110 tok/s to 203.93 tok/s**, an overall increase of approximately
**85%**, or **1.85x**.

This was not one kernel optimization. The result came from three successive
layers:

1. Rework the bandwidth-bound HIP kernels.
2. Replace hundreds of ordinary HIP launches with one retained PM4 replay.
3. Reshape the retained graph to eliminate launches and intermediate memory
   traffic.

The final path remains ordinary autoregressive decoding. It does not depend on
MTP, DFlash, speculative acceptance, clock pinning, or a reduced-correctness
benchmark.

## Phase 1: make the HIP kernel path competitive

The initial profile showed that the hot HFQ4G256 GEMV family occupied **58.38%
of decode kernel time**. The family was already using about 74% of measured DRAM
bandwidth, so the useful transformations were largely about reducing traffic
and amortizing work, not adding arithmetic.

The first controlled win paired adjacent output rows so they could reuse the
input vector:

- Baseline: 125.0 tok/s
- R=2 multirow: 130.3 tok/s
- Improvement: 4.24%
- Register occupancy preserved
- Byte-exact greedy output and coherent sampled output

The autonomous gfx1201 kernel loop then found a composition of memory-side
wins:

- Pair adjacent residual rows and reuse `x` and sigmoid results.
- Fold RMSNorm work into the MQ rotation load path.
- Pair MoE down rows and reuse the rotated expert activation.
- Improve top-k, Gated DeltaNet, gate/up, residual, grouped MoE and QKVZA
  kernels.
- Replace the attention hot path with the winning gfx1201 tile shape.

The first major rollover (`35811504e`) reported **+14.75% composed**. A
subsequent attention checkpoint (`77e1dfe4b`) added another **+4.26%**. By July
9 the measured baseline had reached approximately **153–157 tok/s**, and the
complete default winning composition reached approximately **162 tok/s**.

Those kernel wins were eventually landed as a clean, gfx1201-isolated set in
PR #522 (`db492c9cf`) rather than depending on the experimental autoresearch
branch.

## Phase 2: retained PM4 replay

At 162 tok/s, launch and synchronization overhead had become large enough to
attack directly.

One A3B decode position contained:

- 833 kernel launches
- 26 unique kernels
- 832 inter-launch boundaries

Redline records that fixed launch sequence and lowers it into one retained
gfx12 PM4 indirect buffer. The command processor replays the buffer through a
public ROCr vendor AQL packet, avoiding hundreds of ordinary HIP launch
operations per token.

The first conservative A3B replay measured:

- Tuned HipGraph: 164.220 tok/s
- Retained PM4: 174.087 tok/s
- Improvement: 6.01%

After proving that the shared-expert down path and routed-expert gate/up path
had disjoint live resources, Redline removed one unnecessary intermediate wait
per layer:

- Tuned HipGraph median: 165.839 tok/s
- Retained PM4 median: 178.320 tok/s
- Improvement: 7.53%

The retained tape remained single-stream. Direct AQL replay and two-queue
variants were explored, but neither beat the single-stream retained-PM4 path
end to end.

## Phase 3: make the retained tape lean

Once retained replay worked, the next gains came from treating the tape as a
program rather than as a literal copy of HIP's launch behavior.

Redline progressively added:

- Dependency-derived waits instead of conservative waits at every boundary.
- Retention of invariant shader registers between repeated kernel launches.
- gfx12-specific GCR and cache-action encoding.
- Buffer-SRD weight loads in suitable GEMV families.
- Full launch-register dirty gating.
- Boundary-only cache synchronization.

Representative measured steps included:

- Removing 50 unnecessary waits: **168.624 -> 172.567 tok/s**, +2.34%.
- Retaining invariant register state: PM4 tape size reduced by 30.37%, with a
  small long-context gain.
- Buffer loads for QKVZA, QKV and MoE down: approximately +1%.
- gfx12 GCR trimming (`cc8546a61`): approximately **+2.1–2.6%** in 30-row
  A/B tests.
- Full PM4 dirty gating (`9c4692414`): approximately **+0.3–0.7%**.

This brought the short-context autoregressive path into the **194–195 tok/s**
range.

The important result was not "remove every fence." Blanket L2-skip and several
more aggressive policies were neutral or negative. The winning policy retained
only the synchronization justified by the actual allocation and dependency
graph.

## Phase 4: reshape the graph

With the dispatch substrate lean, Redline began eliminating launches and
intermediate buffers in the existing decode graph.

### Fused wave64 router

The router was fused into a wave64 kernel and removed one launch per MoE layer
(`f55ec99e1`):

- Tape: 833 -> 793 dispatches
- Best TG128 result: 195.53 tok/s
- Expert-index mismatches: 0/640
- Maximum routing-weight absolute drift: `2.88e-4`

### Compact DeltaNet Q/K

Pairs of recurrent-state heads were mapped directly to one compact Q/K head,
removing 30 `repeat_interleave` launches (`403efe37f`):

- Tape: 793 -> 763 dispatches
- Result: 196.677 tok/s
- Improvement over bracketing controls: approximately 0.40%
- All 40 materialized-versus-compact hidden states matched byte-for-byte at the
  certification position

### Fused DeltaNet convolution and Q/K normalization

The decode convolution, SiLU, and Q/K L2 normalization were fused on the
gfx1201 Q8 path (`63a606ca7`):

- Tape: 763 -> 733 dispatches
- Controls: 196.520 and 198.212 tok/s
- Candidate: 201.174 tok/s
- Improvement over the mean control: approximately 1.93%
- Sampled eight-turn decode: 167.8 versus 164.6 tok/s

This was the step that established a repeatable result above 200 tok/s.

## Product result

The retained PM4 route is now selected automatically for the narrow
configuration it was certified for:

- Single GPU
- `gfx12`
- Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B
- `.mq4r`
- Ordinary AR

Explicit replay settings retain precedence, and model changes reset the
retained state. If the graph cannot be recognized or certified, the path fails
closed to ordinary HIP execution.

## Correctness and measurement discipline

Several attractive-looking results were rejected during the campaign. That
prevented invalid 200+ measurements from entering the winning line.

The final process required:

- Automatic clocks; manual pinning was found to reduce utilization.
- At least ten warmup rows before measuring retained replay.
- Reversed A/B ordering for small wins.
- Fifteen-position HIP-versus-PM4 shadow checks.
- Logits, KV-cache and recurrent-state validation.
- An eight-turn sampled serving battery with recall checks.
- gfx1100 isolation for gfx1201-specific kernel changes.
- Fresh artifact and sidecar validation.

Two particularly useful failures were:

- An apparent wave64 LM-head result near 200 tok/s was invalid because odd
  vocabulary rows were skipped.
- A later 205 tok/s candidate used a stale sidecar and was rejected by the
  shadow gate.

Other negative experiments included direct AQL replay, dual-queue replay,
blanket L2 eviction changes, dispatch-interleave changes, broad wave64
conversions, and several fusions that reduced launch count but regressed total
execution time.

## What Redline contributed

The final 204 tok/s result is not solely a faster kernel and not solely a
lower-overhead launcher.

Redline supplied a full optimization stack:

1. A measured kernel experiment loop that raised tuned HIP from roughly 110 to
   162 tok/s.
2. A retained-PM4 backend that removed per-launch HIP overhead.
3. Dependency-aware fence and wait generation.
4. Cache and launch-state retention specialized for gfx12.
5. Graph shaping that reduced the decode tape from 833 to 733 dispatches.
6. Correctness gates strong enough to reject attractive but invalid 200+
   results.
7. A fail-closed product policy that enables the winning route automatically
   only where it was certified.

The resulting improvement is approximately **1.85x** over the starting gfx12
MQ4R implementation, while preserving ordinary autoregressive behavior and
coherent long-context serving.
